What you need to know before you buy or build

Concise guidance from our team on the practical realities of Mauritian real estate in 2026.

Buying as a foreigner

Foreigners can legally own property in Mauritius, but not on the open market. Approved routes:

  • PDS / Smart City / IRS / RES — branded residences and integrated developments.
  • G+2 apartments — minimum Rs 6M, buyer must be non-citizen for at least 5 years.
  • Resale of existing foreign-owned units under an approved scheme.

Purchases of USD 375,000+ under an approved scheme can grant a residence permit for the buyer, spouse, dependent children, and parents.

The buying process

A standard transaction involves five clear stages:

  • 1. Offer & agreement — negotiate price and conditions.
  • 2. Promise of sale — binding pre-contract, deposit paid.
  • 3. Scheme approval (foreigners) — EDB application if applicable.
  • 4. Notarial deed — prepared by a Notary Public.
  • 5. Registration & duty — 5% registration duty (10% for some schemes in 2026).

Taxes & ownership costs

  • No capital gains tax on property resale.
  • No inheritance tax for non-residents.
  • 15% flat income tax, with double-tax treaties (FR, UK, ZA, IN, etc.).
  • Registration duty — 5% (10% for certain 2026 scheme purchases).
  • Annual land tax — modest, based on land value.
  • Syndic / maintenance — varies by residence, typically Rs 1,000–5,000/month.

Building in Mauritius

If you own land, you can build — but the process matters:

  • Building permit required from the local district council.
  • Land use — confirm residential zoning before purchase.
  • Construction cost — typically Rs 25,000–50,000 per m² (standard to luxury).
  • Timeline — 8–14 months for a typical 3-bedroom home.
  • Contractor selection — always verify references and past projects.

04 — Buying Guide

Buying a villa in Mauritius as a foreigner: the rules

Mauritius is one of a small number of jurisdictions where a foreigner can hold freehold property outright and use that purchase to secure residence for the whole family. It is also a market with real restrictions: a non-citizen cannot simply buy any house or plot on the open market.

Core rule: non-citizens may purchase residential property only through an EDB-approved scheme or a qualifying G+2 apartment. Freehold land and standard houses on the open market remain reserved for Mauritian citizens.
Scheme What it is Residency? Typical Use
PDS — Property Development Scheme The main modern framework for new developments; replaced IRS and RES for new projects. Buyers own freehold, can rent out, and repatriate proceeds. Yes, at USD 375k+ Primary route for villa buyers seeking residency or a managed second home
Smart City Scheme Mixed-use live-work developments blending residential, commercial, and innovation hubs. Yes, at USD 375k+ Buyers wanting urban convenience and long-let potential
G+2 Apartments in buildings of at least ground plus two upper floors, priced from MUR 6 million. No Lower entry point; investment or second home without residency
IRS / RES (legacy) Predecessors to PDS; no new projects approved, but an active resale market. Yes, on qualifying purchases Resale villas within established resort estates
IHS — Invest Hotel Scheme Serviced unit within a hotel development, often branded. Yes, on qualifying purchases Hands-off rental investment with hotel management

Step-by-step purchase sequence

  • Confirm your eligibility route (PDS, Smart City, G+2, or legacy resale).
  • Select a licensed notary — mandatory for conveyancing.
  • Submit an application to the Economic Development Board where required.
  • Sign the compromis de vente (preliminary sale agreement).
  • Complete due diligence: title, charges, planning, and developer standing.
  • Transfer funds from overseas in a hard convertible currency or MUR.
  • Sign the deed of sale before the notary and register with the Registrar-General.
  • Apply for the residence permit if the purchase meets the threshold.
Residency detail: a purchase of at least USD 375,000 under an EDB-approved scheme grants a residence permit to the buyer, their spouse or partner, dependent children up to 24, and dependent parents — valid for as long as the property is owned, with no minimum stay requirement.
